What Are the Drawbacks to Staying Connected?

Every post, message, and piece of content watched can create data. How many types of data do major social media apps say they collect?

Explore the data
01

Background

What qualifies as personal data?

Personal data can include obvious information such as email addresses, current location, photos, and contacts. It can also include less visible information such as device identifiers, search history, diagnostics, and app usage.

Why do platforms collect it?

Platforms use data to operate features, recommend personalized content, show relevant advertising, measure engagement, and improve their products. Collecting large amounts of data can also create privacy risks, especially when users do not fully understand how that data may be used.

Being connected does not only describe people. It can also refer to the connections among accounts, devices, advertisers, and behavioral profiles.

03

Findings & Reflection

1

Collection is broad

Most platforms disclose multiple categories, not only the information users actively post.

2

Labels need context

A label says data may be handled; it does not reveal collection frequency, retention time, or every downstream use.

3

Privacy is a trade-off

Convenience and personalization can be useful, but users should decide whether each permission is necessary.

My reflection

The most surprising finding was how many categories extend beyond what a person intentionally posts. Identifiers, diagnostics, usage patterns, and location can reveal behavior even when a user shares very little publicly. I also learned that privacy labels are useful but incomplete because they are developer-reported and can change by region, age, and feature.

My conclusion is that the drawback to staying connected is not one single piece of data. It is the combination of many small signals into a detailed profile. Users can reduce exposure by reviewing permissions, turning off unnecessary location access, avoiding contact syncing when it is not needed, limiting ad tracking, and periodically deleting unused accounts.

Sources & Method

Method: I recorded broad data categories disclosed in the U.S. Apple App Store privacy labels for selected apps, then compared the number and sensitivity of those categories. App-store disclosures may change and are not independently verified by Apple.
